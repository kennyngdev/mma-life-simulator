# 拳途人生 Cage Life — Threads Promotion Specification

- Status: accepted promotion baseline
- Version: 1.1.0
- Last updated: 2026-08-30
- Account: `@play_cagelife`
- Primary destination: `https://playcagelife.com/`

## 1. Purpose

Threads promotion exists to recruit the right early players, learn from their play, and steadily build a Traditional-Chinese community around `拳途人生 Cage Life` while the game is still in development.

The account must not present the current build as complete. Promotion should make early participation feel valuable: players are not merely consuming a finished product; their observations help decide what gets clarified, balanced, expanded, or removed.

The order of priorities is:

1. Obtain specific, reproducible player feedback.
2. Bring qualified players to the live PWA.
3. Build recurring conversation with MMA, life-sim, and indie-game audiences.
4. Grow followers and reach as downstream outcomes of the first three goals.

Follower count alone is not a success criterion.

## 2. Audience

### 2.1 Primary audiences

- Traditional-Chinese players in Taiwan, Hong Kong, and the wider Chinese-speaking diaspora.
- Players who enjoy life simulators, career simulators, management games, roguelite runs, and choice-driven biographies.
- MMA and combat-sports fans interested in career decisions, training, injuries, tactics, ranking, and legacy—not only real-time fighting.
- Browser and mobile players willing to test an unfinished indie game and explain where they became interested, confused, or disengaged.

### 2.2 Secondary audiences

- Traditional-Chinese indie developers and small studios.
- Game-design, UI/UX, localization, PWA, and web-game creators.
- Indie-game curators and accounts that regularly spotlight playable prototypes.

### 2.3 Audience qualification

A qualified visitor does at least one of the following:

- opens the game and reaches character creation;
- completes the first meaningful career decision;
- reports a bug with enough context to investigate;
- explains what was compelling, confusing, slow, or missing;
- returns for a later build or discusses the game with another player.

## 3. Positioning and voice

### 3.1 Core promise

> 這不只是一場拳賽，而是一名拳手從無名到退役的人生。

Supporting promise:

> 每一次訓練、對手與人生抉擇，都會留下痕跡。

### 3.2 Voice

All public game-account posts are written primarily in Traditional Chinese. English may appear only where it is already part of the product name, MMA terminology, a quoted player term, or a deliberate attempt to reach a secondary international audience.

The voice is:

- candid about the unfinished state;
- curious and specific when asking questions;
- confident about the game's premise without claiming unproven quality;
- conversational rather than corporate;
- lightly playful when the subject supports it;
- respectful toward players, fighters, other developers, and competing games.

Avoid generic advertising phrases such as “史上最強,” “革命性,” or “不容錯過.” Do not fabricate testimonials, player counts, awards, partnerships, or scarcity.

## 4. Promotion funnel

```text
useful or intriguing Threads post
→ conversation, reply, repost, or profile visit
→ playcagelife.com
→ character creation and first career decisions
→ reply or DM with an observation
→ acknowledged and categorized feedback
→ visible improvement or follow-up question
→ returning tester and community member
```

Every promotional post should serve one main stage of this funnel. Not every post needs a site link. Conversation posts may optimize for replies; playtest posts may optimize for qualified clicks; update posts may close the loop with people who previously gave feedback.

## 5. Content pillars

Use these proportions as a four-week planning guide, not a rigid per-week quota.

| Pillar | Share | Purpose | Example |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Playable moments and fighter stories | 30% | Demonstrate the life-sim fantasy through specific outcomes | “你的第一位拳手因傷提前退役，還是一路打到世界舞台？” |
| Development decisions and progress | 25% | Make development legible and give followers a reason to return | Before/after UI, ranking changes, new career consequences |
| Focused feedback requests | 25% | Generate actionable evidence for the next iteration | One screenshot plus one question about clarity or motivation |
| MMA and life-sim design conversation | 10% | Reach adjacent communities through genuine perspective | Why fight count should not secretly decide retirement |
| Personality, humor, and milestones | 10% | Humanize the project without turning the account into filler | A surprising seeded fighter, strange career outcome, build milestone |

## 6. Publishing cadence

### 6.1 Baseline week

Publish four original posts per week:

- **Monday — Development note:** one concrete change, its reason, and the player problem it addresses.
- **Wednesday — Design question:** one narrow question that can be answered without playing for hours.
- **Friday — Playable proof:** screenshot, short clip, or compact career story with a direct play link.
- **Sunday — Community or challenge post:** invite players to compare seeds, careers, builds, or outcomes.

Maintain at least one weekend post. If four strong posts are not available, publish three strong posts instead of filling the schedule with generic promotion. Never exceed two original promotional posts in one day unless responding to a live event or a major release.

### 6.2 Conversation work

- Spend 15–20 minutes on Threads on publishing days before or after posting.
- Write at least five substantive replies per week on relevant indie-game, web-game, life-sim, game-design, or MMA discussions.
- Reply as a participant with a relevant observation; do not drop the game link into unrelated conversations.
- Acknowledge direct player feedback within 24 hours when practical.
- Ask one follow-up question when the original feedback lacks the context needed to act.

## 7. Post construction

### 7.1 Default structure

1. **Hook:** one direct sentence that creates curiosity, tension, recognition, or a clear question.
2. **Proof:** a screenshot, clip, concrete mechanic, career outcome, or development decision.
3. **Context:** why this matters to the player's experience.
4. **One primary CTA:** play, answer, compare, or report—not several at once.

Keep the opening understandable before the “more” fold. Use short paragraphs and visible breathing room. When media is included, the text must explain what the viewer should notice rather than repeating the image.

### 7.2 Topics and discovery

- Add at least one accurate Threads topic tag to every original post, prioritizing indie games, game development, MMA, browser games, or life simulation. Do not publish an original post without a topic tag.
- Use hashtags sparingly. A short cluster is acceptable for a launch or milestone, but tagged topics and natural language are preferred for routine posts.
- Create original Threads-native copy. Do not mechanically copy an Instagram caption.
- When eligible, allow useful posts to be recommended on Instagram or Facebook unless a specific post should remain Threads-only.

### 7.3 Visual proof requirement

- Attach at least one current game screenshot, short clip, or other game visual to every original Threads post.
- The media must show the specific feature, screen, change, or player situation discussed in the post. For an update post, favor a before/after pair or a screenshot of the changed result; do not attach generic art as a substitute for proof.
- Capture media from the current live build whenever possible. If a change cannot yet be shown in the live build, label the visual as in development and state that it is not live.
- Write the post text so readers know what to look for in the attached media. Use an accessible crop and ensure Traditional-Chinese UI text remains legible on a phone.

### 7.4 Link policy

- Keep `https://playcagelife.com/` in the profile links and bio.
- Include the direct game link in playable-build, launch, major-update, and explicit playtest-recruitment posts.
- Conversation-first posts may omit the link and rely on the profile.
- Use the canonical HTTPS URL; do not promote temporary deployment URLs.

## 8. Feedback system

### 8.1 Questions that produce useful answers

Prefer one focused prompt per post:

- “建立拳手時，哪個選項最難理解？”
- “完成第一場比賽後，你最想立刻做什麼？”
- “你在哪一刻開始覺得這名拳手是『你的』？”
- “哪個畫面讓你不知道下一步要按哪裡？”
- “你會因為什麼理由再開第二段生涯？”

Avoid “大家覺得如何？” as the only question.

### 8.2 Bug-report request

For reproducible problems, request only the minimum useful context:

- what the player was trying to do;
- what happened and what they expected;
- device and browser;
- game version when visible;
- Seed and current career stage when relevant;
- screenshot or short recording when practical.

Do not request passwords, private account data, full browser histories, or unrelated personal information.

### 8.3 Feedback categories

Classify each actionable report as one primary category:

- onboarding / character creation;
- navigation / UI clarity;
- pacing / motivation;
- progression / training;
- combat / tactics;
- balance / economy;
- biography / narrative consequence;
- save / PWA / device behavior;
- bug / performance;
- content or localization.

Record whether the item is a blocker, recurring friction, preference, idea, or positive signal. Similar reports should be grouped rather than counted as unrelated requests.

### 8.4 Closing the loop

When feedback leads to a visible change, publish a concise follow-up:

```text
你們上週提到＿＿＿＿。
這次版本改成＿＿＿＿，因為＿＿＿＿。
如果再玩一次，想知道這一步現在是否更清楚。
```

Never promise that every suggestion will ship. Thank the player, summarize the underlying issue, and state whether the team is investigating, testing, accepting, or declining it.

## 9. Four-week starting campaign

### Week 1 — What kind of life is this?

- Keep the existing launch/playtest post as the account's primary introduction and pin it if pinning is available.
- Post one seeded fighter reveal and ask which starting route followers would choose.
- Explain in one short post why the game is a life simulator rather than only a fight simulator.
- Ask early players where they first became confused.

### Week 2 — Build a fighter identity

- Show the three starting experiences and ask which fantasy is strongest.
- Share one training or learned-move decision with a screenshot.
- Post a short career outcome that could only happen because of an earlier decision.
- Invite players to share a Seed and compare results.

### Week 3 — Make the first session better

- Focus feedback on character creation, the first offer, and the first camp.
- Share one before/after change produced by player feedback.
- Ask one MMA audience question that directly relates to a game decision.
- Publish one play link with a 10–15 minute testing mission.

### Week 4 — Retention and biography

- Show a retirement biography or meaningful failure without spoiling every outcome.
- Ask what would motivate a second career.
- Summarize the month's most useful lessons and what will be tested next.
- Invite previous testers back for the changed build.

## 10. Initial post backlog

These are premises, not final copy:

1. “普通人、業餘愛好者、半職業選手——你會從哪裡開始？”
2. “一名拳手最難承受的是失敗、受傷，還是知道自己時間不多？”
3. Show a generated fighter and ask followers to predict the career.
4. Explain one decision removed from the game because it was not fun or clear.
5. Ask whether the first opponent offer communicates risk well enough.
6. Share a move-learning choice and ask which two followers would take.
7. Compare two careers created from the same Seed with different choices.
8. Share an unexpected retirement biography.
9. Ask players to complete only character creation and report the hardest decision.
10. Ask MMA fans which part of fight preparation games usually miss.
11. Publish a PWA add-to-home-screen reminder tied to a new build.
12. Thank testers and name three changes made from their feedback.

## 11. Measurement

### 11.1 Weekly process metrics

- original posts published;
- substantive replies written on other accounts;
- player-feedback replies acknowledged within 24 hours;
- one completed Insights review;
- one documented learning applied to the next week's plan.

### 11.2 Outcome metrics

Review these in Threads Insights over rolling 7-, 30-, and 90-day windows where available:

- views per post and total views;
- replies, quotes, reposts, and likes;
- follower growth and audience geography;
- profile visits and game-link clicks;
- discovery source, including recommendations on other Meta apps;
- actionable feedback items received;
- returning testers identified through conversation.

For the first two weeks, establish a baseline. Afterward, compare posts by pillar and format rather than chasing one viral outlier. The primary 30-day outcome target is at least ten actionable player-feedback items, with at least three leading to a confirmed change, experiment, or explicit design decision.

### 11.3 Experiment log

For each meaningful post, record:

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| Date/time | 2026-09-02 20:00 JST |
| Pillar | Focused feedback request |
| Hook | “建立拳手時，哪個選項最難選？” |
| Format | Text + screenshot |
| Topic | Game development |
| CTA | Reply with one confusing choice |
| Views / interactions | From Insights after 48 hours |
| Link clicks | From Insights when present |
| Actionable feedback | Count plus category |
| Learning | Short posts with one question produced more specific replies |

Change one main variable at a time—hook, format, topic, CTA, or posting time—and evaluate over at least two comparable posts.

## 12. Moderation and operating rules

- The account remains public while promotion is active.
- Criticism is evidence, not an invitation to argue. Clarify, thank, and investigate.
- Hide, restrict, or report harassment, impersonation, scams, doxxing, or unsafe links.
- Do not quote-post a small account merely to expose or ridicule it.
- Do not mass-follow, follow/unfollow, buy followers, automate unsolicited replies, or send cold promotional DMs.
- Do not post confidential credentials, private player data, unreleased personal information, or raw debugging output.
- Public posts or scheduled posts require owner review unless the owner explicitly requests direct publication in the current task.
- If a post makes a factual product claim, verify it against the current live build and accepted design specification before publishing.

## 13. Weekly operating checklist

1. Review the prior seven days in Threads Insights.
2. Read unresolved player feedback and current development priorities.
3. Choose four posts across at least three content pillars.
4. Draft each post with one primary goal and one primary CTA.
5. Prepare accurate screenshots or clips from the current live build, attach one to every original post, and add an accurate Threads topic tag.
6. Schedule where useful, leaving room for timely conversation.
7. Engage with replies and relevant community discussions.
8. Categorize feedback and route it to the appropriate design or implementation work.
9. Publish at least one feedback-loop closure when a player report caused a change.
10. Record the week's learning and adjust the next plan.

## 14. Platform basis and review cadence

This plan uses current official Threads guidance:

- Meta recommends approximately 2–5 posts per week for audience growth, reports that replies account for a substantial share of views, and recommends original Threads-specific posts, weekend activity, conversational prompts, and explanatory text with media.
- Tagged topics generally receive more views than posts without a topic.
- Threads Insights provides post, interaction, follower, discovery, and link-performance data over time.
- Threads supports profile links, drafts, and scheduling, though availability and placement may vary by account and device.

Review this specification quarterly or whenever Threads materially changes topics, communities, recommendations, analytics, scheduling, or link behavior.
